Did more people find your page after the change?

First ask what you want to know: did someone see your page in search, was it cited in an answer, or did someone actually visit? These are different events. Check what your Google, Bing or site reports really show, and leave unavailable information unknown.

Google: is your page appearing in search?

Google introduced dedicated generative-AI performance views for Search and Discover and stated that the rollout reached all websites worldwide on 31 August 2026. If the relevant report is available for your property, choose a page and a period and write down what it shows. Keep general Search and AI-related appearances separate where the reports overlap. Changing the page's HTML by itself does not prove visibility.

Bing: is your page being cited?

Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance is documented as a public-preview reporting surface for citation activity across supported Microsoft AI experiences. If your property has the AI Performance report, note which pages were cited and the dates covered. Do not count citations as visits or pretend the report includes every answer.

ChatGPT: did someone actually visit?

OpenAI states that ChatGPT automatically includes utm_source=chatgpt.com in referral URLs. If your site statistics are set up with appropriate permissions, check visits associated with that source. Note the landing page and any useful next step you can legitimately observe. This measures visits, not every answer that may have mentioned your site.

Try this with one page before changing the whole site

  1. Pick a page. Choose one guide that should help a particular reader, and write down the problem you want to fix.
  2. Record the starting point. Save the searches and visits you can observe, including the exact dates. Mark missing data as unavailable, not zero.
  3. Make one clear change. Repair an internal link, clarify an answer or add a genuine example. Record what you changed and when.
  4. Check the live page. Confirm your change actually appears and everything still works. That is not yet evidence of more visitors.
  5. Compare sensible periods. Allow for reporting delays and compare complete periods. Look at real counts as well as percentages; use a similar unchanged page as context where possible.
  6. Ask what else could explain it. Seasonality, other site changes and demand can move the numbers. Do not assume your edit caused the difference.

Keep a small record of what happened

Search: non-brand impressions, clicks, indexed priority pages. Google generative: generative-AI impressions/pages. Bing: citation activity and cited pages. ChatGPT: sessions from utm_source=chatgpt.com. Business: one meaningful action such as signup, download, contact or tool use.

A finished edit is not a successful outcome by itself. If reports are unavailable, say so. If visitors increased but useful actions did not, record both observations.
