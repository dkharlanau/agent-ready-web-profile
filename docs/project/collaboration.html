Collaboration disclosure rules

  • Identify whether a contribution is independent, owner-controlled, sponsored, paid, reciprocal or otherwise commercially connected when that relationship can affect interpretation.
  • Do not turn a partner logo, quote or implementation into evidence of ranking, citation or universal product effectiveness.
  • Keep source attribution and experimental limitations attached when contributed evidence enters Goose research or recommendation work.
  • Do not imply that a contributor, customer, partner or cited company is an employee, agent or endorser unless that relationship is explicitly established.
  • Commercial influence must not silently promote a recommendation from hypothesis to proven pattern.

What makes a useful proposal

A strong collaboration request names the site or system, the user problem, what already exists, the proposed change, who owns the relevant data or code, what can be published, what must remain private and how success or failure could be checked.

Prefer one falsifiable collaboration over ten vague partnerships. Goose is designed to learn from scoped implementation and evidence, not from a growing logo wall.
