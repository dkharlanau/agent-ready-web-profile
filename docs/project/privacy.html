PRIVACY / OPTIONAL MEASUREMENT / PUBLIC DATA

Measure less. Explain more.

Goose uses a small optional measurement layer to understand which pages people actually use. It stays off until a visitor chooses to allow it. This page explains the choice without turning privacy into a technical manual.

Current intent

Measurement: optional, opt-in

Public reporting: aggregate only

Unknown ≠ zero

Optional site measurement

When the choice appears, no measurement request is sent until a visitor chooses “Allow.” The choice is remembered in the browser so the question does not need to appear on every visit.

What the repository can and cannot show. The source can show when measurement is allowed to start and what the site intends to send. It cannot prove every setting or behavior outside the site itself.

What we try not to collect or publish

  • No visitor-level measurement export should be published by the project.
  • Public reporting should stay aggregate.
  • Unknown results stay unknown instead of being turned into zero.
  • Raw search queries, free-text submissions and submitted audit URLs should not appear in public evidence files.
  • Private reports should be reduced to the few fields needed for a defined question or decision.

Remembering your choice

Your choice is saved in this browser so we do not keep asking. Clearing site data can remove it. That preference applies only to this optional site measurement; it is not permission for unrelated uses.

External services and links

Some measurement and linked services are operated by third parties under their own terms and privacy practices. Following an external link leaves the Goose project site. A partnership link does not make that external service part of the Goose privacy boundary.

When this page must change

Update this page when the product adds forms, accounts, payments, a new measurement service, a different consent choice, meaningful new browser storage, personal-data collection or another important use of visitor data. The explanation should change when the product changes.

What this page does not claim

This is a factual project-data-use summary, not a jurisdiction-specific legal compliance statement. It does not claim GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA or another regime is satisfied merely because a consent banner or privacy page exists.
