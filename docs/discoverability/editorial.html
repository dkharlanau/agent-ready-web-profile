001

Write pages people can use

Answer the reader's question before explaining your process

People shouldn't have to read a long introduction to learn whether a page is for them.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • On a decision page, give the practical answer near the top and say who it applies to.
  • State the one or two conditions that would change your advice. Keep a story-first opening when the story itself is the point.

Check the change

  • A reviewer can locate the answer, audience and main exception early in the reading path; no universal viewport or paragraph quota is required.

What result to look for

Ask a reader to find the recommendation and explain when they would choose differently.

Why it may help: A precise opening may improve understanding and qualified engagement.

Watch out for: Universal advice disguised as a confident one-line answer.

Write pages people can use

Show one example someone can follow

'Follow best practices' doesn't tell a visitor what to do on Monday morning.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Choose a concrete task and provide realistic, safe starting information.
  • Show the steps, the result and a mistake someone could actually make. Clearly label invented teaching data.
  • Check that a reader unfamiliar with the author can repeat the example.

Check the change

  • A fresh run reproduces the documented result within stated tolerances.

What result to look for

See whether a reader can complete the example and explain what to do if it fails.

Why it may help: Concrete examples may earn references and reduce evaluation friction.

Watch out for: Presenting an invented execution transcript as a real run.

Write a title the page can deliver on

A clever headline is not helpful when the content answers a smaller or different question.

v1.1.0documentedPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Name the actual task, decision or result; include versions only when they change the answer.
  • Read the title, main heading and opening together to check that they promise the same thing.
  • Preview smaller screens; there is no universal 60-character ranking rule.

Check the change

  • Ask a reader what they expect from the title, then check whether the page delivers it.

What result to look for

Record whether readers find the promised answer and whether search visitors were misled.

Why it may help: A clearer promise may attract visitors whose needs the page actually serves. Search and AI recommendation effects remain unmeasured.
