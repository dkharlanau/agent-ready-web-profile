001

Know whether a change helped

Take a snapshot before changing your site

If you only look at results afterwards, it's hard to tell what actually changed.

v1.1.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Choose the pages you're trying to improve. Record what is live today and the date of the check.
  • If you can access your search reports, save the available clicks and impressions for those pages. Record the period, timezone and any missing data.
  • Keep dedicated AI-search reports separate from ordinary Search results. One report cannot stand in for another.

Check the change

  • Each baseline metric has a source, date range, and explicit missing-data state.

What result to look for

For each chosen page, mark which starting measurements you have, which are missing and the dates they cover.

Why it may help: A consistent baseline may make later decisions more reliable.

Watch out for: Backfilling invented zeros for unavailable accounts or dates.

Know whether a change helped

Decide what a good result would look like

A chart can look impressive when nobody has agreed what its numbers mean.

v1.1.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Pick one or two outcomes that fit the page: a relevant visit, a useful resource click or a qualified enquiry.
  • For every number, write down what is counted, where it comes from, the date range and any denominator used.
  • Keep searches, citations, visits and completed actions separate. Don't combine figures from reports that count different things.

Check the change

  • A second analyst can recompute each derived value from retained counts.

What result to look for

Check whether another person can reproduce each reported number from the same source and date range.

Why it may help: Stable definitions may prevent misleading benchmark comparisons.

Watch out for: Combining citation counts and clicks into an unexplained visibility score.

Know whether a change helped

See which searches bring the right visitors

A rise in total clicks may only mean more people searched for your name.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Use your available search report to separate brand-name searches from other relevant questions.
  • Compare like-for-like pages, countries and devices only where the report provides those fields.
  • Record missing queries and incomplete report coverage. Don't invent segments by joining incompatible reports.

Check the change

  • Segment totals explain documented differences without pretending query rows are exhaustive.

What result to look for

Compare relevant clicks and impressions for the same page groups and time periods, noting what the report does not show.

Why it may help: Segmentation may locate useful gains or regressions.

Watch out for: Calling every query without the exact brand spelling non-brand.

005

Know whether a change helped

Tell reader visits from crawler traffic

An automated request isn't a person who chose to open your website.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • In your authorized visit reports, identify referrals from known AI services using a maintained list of hosts.
  • Leave traffic you cannot attribute in an unknown group rather than guessing its origin.
  • Keep visits, first-time users and button clicks distinct. A campaign label is a clue about origin, not independent proof.

Check the change

  • Session attribution, verified crawler logs and unclassified observations are separate. A referral host or campaign label does not prove a human visit or establish complete AI traffic coverage.

What result to look for

Count identifiable referred visits, then check meaningful actions within the same properly measured group.

Why it may help: Referral tracking may identify useful downstream demand.

Watch out for: Assuming all direct traffic is AI-driven or every crawler visit is a user.

Know whether a change helped

Look for an action that actually matters

More impressions aren't much help if visitors still cannot complete their task.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Choose a useful next step for the page: opening a worked example, downloading a resource or sending a qualified enquiry.
  • Check that the action really works before counting clicks or events.
  • Record what permission and tracking coverage allow you to observe. A click isn't automatically a completed task.

Check the change

  • An authorized test action follows the defined counting rule; debug receipt, production reporting and business qualification are verified separately. Duplicate sends and missing observations are reported.

What result to look for

Report the observed number of useful actions alongside comparable measured visits, without inventing a rate for missing data.

Why it may help: Outcome measurement may prevent optimizing visibility without usefulness.

Watch out for: Treating every pageview as a conversion.

Check whether your pages are actually listed in search

A page can be technically ready for indexing without appearing in search results.

v1.2.0inferredPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

Why it may help: Separated states may make operational reports more truthful.

Watch out for: Reporting a 100 percent SEO score as 100 percent indexed.

Know whether a change helped

Repeat AI answer checks before drawing conclusions

One answer that mentions your site does not prove people are regularly being recommended to visit it.

v1.2.0experimentalPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Choose a small, fixed set of real questions your audience might ask, including questions without your brand name.
  • Record the service, date, language, location if controllable, answer and actual linked sources for each check.
  • Repeat checks under stated conditions and keep unfavorable or missing mentions. This is a sample, not a market-wide probability.

Check the change

  • Results retain failures and distinguish cited, mentioned, recommended, and absent states.

What result to look for

Show the number of runs, mentions and correct references per service and question, including variation between runs.

Why it may help: A bounded panel may reveal directional answer changes for the sampled conditions.

Watch out for: Cherry-picking screenshots or claiming personalized samples represent market share.

Know whether a change helped

Don't count the same search appearance twice

Adding AI-search impressions to overall Search impressions can count overlapping visibility twice.

v1.1.0documentedPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Label each export with its provider, report type, property, dates and available dimensions.
  • Where the dedicated AI view overlaps general Search reporting, show the two figures separately instead of adding them.
  • Keep Search and Discover separate, and leave fields you cannot observe blank.

Check the change

  • Check compatible dimensions and complete periods; do not derive AI CTR without corresponding supported click data.

What result to look for

Present the separate figures and confirm that no combined total double-counts overlapping observations.

Why it may help: Keeping overlapping reports distinct may prevent inflated visibility claims. Search and AI recommendation effects remain unmeasured.

Watch out for: Turning this scoped practice into a universal ranking rule or publishing unsupported claims.

v1.1.0documentedPattern reviewed 8 Sept 2026

What to do

  • Check whether the relevant report exists and is available for the exact property you own.

Why it may help: Explicit report-availability states may prevent missing data from becoming false zeroes. Search and AI recommendation effects remain unmeasured.
