# Names, locales and offers that remain true

Edition 1.0.0 · reviewed 8 September 2026 · corpus 1.6.0.

This review adds ten scoped patterns and a reusable commercial/localization worksheet. Existing title promises, entity identity, reciprocal hreflang, comparison criteria and total-cost practices remain applicable. The new details address failures that broad checklists can miss. This is not a recipe for guaranteed positions in Search or AI recommendations.

[Pattern library](./discoverability.html) · [Anti-patterns](./ANTI-PATTERNS.html) · [Review worksheet and CSV fixtures](https://github.com/dkharlanau/agent-ready-web-profile/tree/main/templates/growth/commercial-localization)

## Name four different things separately

A brand name identifies the entity; a category explains what it is; a page title describes the particular answer; a URL locates it. A title such as “Canonical URLs for Jekyll project sites — Example Brand” is a synthetic illustration of this separation, not a prescribed keyword formula. Preserve real brand spelling while making the title's language and script consistent with the primary content. Google may generate a different title link. [Title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).

Choose readable slugs and consistent case before publication. Do not rename established paths solely to add keywords. If a move is necessary, review the existing migration pattern and the real host's redirect capabilities. [URL guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/url-structure).

## Translate the useful task

A translated menu with an unchanged main answer is not a complete localized experience. Review instructions, examples and commercial facts together. Visible language matters to Google's language identification; the HTML lang attribute still serves accessibility and other consumers. Do not infer that one replaces the other. [Multilingual guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites).

The locale CSV is a planning fixture, not a ready-to-publish hreflang graph. Both rows have pending reviews. First verify that translations and purchasable offers actually exist, then apply the existing reciprocal/self-hreflang and canonical patterns. Do not generate placeholder pages for every country. Product guidance calls for distinct URLs for products sold in different currencies; this is not a mandate to clone informational pages. [Product guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet).

## Model the actual commercial commitment

Make billing units, commitment and material components visible. A monthly equivalent is different from a monthly cancellable contract. In the synthetic offer CSV, the annual plan costs USD 240 prepaid plus USD 60 setup: USD 300 for year one. The monthly plan costs USD 30 × 12: USD 360 for the same horizon. Neither row includes a tax assumption or proves comparable features. The quote-only service remains unknown.

Schema.org UnitPriceSpecification can express billing-related semantics, but its reviewed page is the development vocabulary. Its fields are not automatically supported Google rich-result properties. Consult the actual consuming feature and pin a released vocabulary if the implementation depends on versioned terms. [Vocabulary](https://schema.org/UnitPriceSpecification).

For a service priced by quote, describe the service and the quote process without inventing price zero. “Starting at” needs a real attainable scope and visible conditions. Model the real provider and service; this alone does not grant a dedicated Google service result. [Service vocabulary](https://schema.org/Service).

## Separate software identity, free access and eligibility

An accurate SoftwareApplication graph and Google feature eligibility are different checks. The current documented feature requires a name, offer price and rating or review. If genuine review evidence is absent, record the feature gap; do not invent a review. Zero is appropriate for an app available without payment, with actual free limits accurately described. Test the advertised free task rather than assuming a free download means every described workflow is free. [Software app guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app).

An AggregateOffer describes actual aggregated offers. Do not use it as a substitute for product variants or a decorative price range. Trace bounds and count to their underlying offers. [AggregateOffer guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet).

## Compare competitors on a declared basis

The price-basis pattern extends the existing same-scenario and total-cost practices: retain each original price, observation date, usage level and commitment. Convert only with an explicit rate and date where conversion is actually needed. Unknown competitor terms stay unknown. A lower first-year arithmetic total is not evidence that a product is better for every user.

Use the worksheet to assign the following reviews: title/content consistency, locale task completion, offer facts, pricing calculation, current feature eligibility, competitor evidence, built HTML and published response. Keep each status separate. Measurement follows through the existing Growth Loop; a completed review is not an acquired customer.
