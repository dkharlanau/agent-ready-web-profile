HTML, JSON-LD, or Markdown: which publishing surface comes first?

For maintainers of public documentation · As of 8 September 2026

Our editorial recommendation is to establish a useful canonical page first, add truthful structured facts where they serve a defined consumer, and test an alternate text representation when a supported client benefits from it.

Criteria and method

We compare the reader job served, the evidence each approach can carry, and its practical limitation. The factual descriptions below come from the linked primary documentation. The “use it when” column is an ARWP judgment. We did not conduct a speed, retrieval, or ranking benchmark.

Choose by the actual task

  • The answer is missing or unclear: improve the canonical page and verify it with a reader task.
  • An entity consumer confuses identities: test a small graph with stable IDs and visible factual support.
  • A specific client loses information during HTML extraction: compare a generated Markdown version against the same held-out questions.

These approaches can complement each other. The comparison helps prioritize work; it does not name a universal winner.

Implementation and maintenance cost

Before adding a format, decide its authoritative source and how updates reach it. Use one changed claim to test propagation across the page, graph, and export. Leave unknown performance effects unknown until measured.

Inspect criteria and claims in the comparison receipt. For a page-level pattern, use the readable-answer example.
