# Editorial interview: earn the next useful action

Version 1.0.0 · source review 2026-09-08 · project workflow, not a ranking formula.

Use this brief for one real page. Record its URL, intended reader, locale, owner, corpus version and selected pattern IDs. Mark unanswered questions as open work. Do not generate fictional experience to fill the brief.

| Ask yourself | Save in the brief | Reject this shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is the reader trying to decide or finish? | One task and the action or understanding that completes it | A list of keyword variations presented as separate needs |
| Does the title accurately distinguish this page? | Concrete subject, scope and appropriate product or locale context | A universal character quota copied from another site's house style |
| What must the reader know first? | Answer and boundary for decisions; prerequisites for procedures; appropriate context for explanations | Forcing every genre into the same opening paragraph |
| Why should this explanation exist? | A real example, useful synthesis, verified comparison or distinctive observation | Longer paraphrases of existing search results |
| Which sentence carries the most risk if quoted alone? | Claim, nearby qualification and supporting source passage | A source list with no claim-to-source connection |
| Can someone reproduce the example? | Safe inputs, actual version, procedure, output and known limits | A fabricated terminal transcript or customer story |
| What would change the recommendation? | Relevant counterexample, exclusion or alternative | Decorative disadvantages that never affect the decision |
| What voice helps this reader act? | Direct, respectful wording and consistent terms tested in context | Claiming first person, confidence or humor is a ranking factor |
| Does the locale change the answer? | Reviewed terms, actual availability, currency and constraints | Translating a price or service promise that does not apply locally |
| Which useful asset saves work? | Choose an applicable checklist, worked calculation, reproducible example or data dictionary | Creating a download merely to add an asset badge |
| What happens when this becomes wrong? | Review trigger, owner, correction path and historical release link | Resetting dates or overwriting cited data silently |
| How will we know it helped? | Task comprehension and completion; separately, observed search/citation and qualified actions | Treating publication or schema validity as acquisition success |

## Draft, then challenge

Write the title, opening, section outline and one central claim before producing the whole article. Read them as a first-time visitor. Remove headings that add no distinct job. Put necessary qualifications where the decision is made; supporting depth can follow. Prefer natural language over keyword repetition.

Choose assets by need. A pricing page may benefit from a scenario worksheet with explicit assumptions; documentation may need a runnable example; a dataset needs definitions and coverage. A page can be useful without a download. Keep any application-specific evidence IDs non-sensitive: public HTML is readable even when attributes are not visually displayed.

Record the source passage and what it actually supports. Separate observed results, interpretation and illustrative examples. Preserve a failed example with its status rather than reporting it as a successful run. Recheck quotations and current product claims before publication. These are manual editorial judgments, not an automatic quality score.

## Evidence and limits

[Google's people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports usefulness, original value and appropriate production transparency. The brief format and interview are project methods.

[GitHub's article guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/contributing/style-guide-and-content-model/contents-of-a-github-docs-article) provides an editorial precedent for clear titles and prerequisites before procedures. Its layout limits are site-specific.

[Google's developer voice guidance](https://developers.google.com/style/tone) supports approachable, direct language for technical readers. Apply it with regard to genre and locale; no universally winning search voice is established here.

[W3C data publishing guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/dwbp/) supports provenance and version history for data. Extending that discipline to editorial claims is an explicit project inference.
